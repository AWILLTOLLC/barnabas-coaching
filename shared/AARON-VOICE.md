# Aaron's Voice — Writing on His Behalf

*Source: Maven marketing channel observations, 2026-03-20. Derived from Aaron's own writing (Reddit post, r/amateurradio, Morse Command launch) and direct corrections during copy review.*

Use this when writing publicly for Aaron: social posts, emails, blog content, product announcements, outreach, anything that goes out under his name or his businesses.

For your own agent voice, use the rules in IDENTITY.md / shared/VOICE.md. This file is for *his* voice specifically.

---

## The Register

Direct. Informal. Technically specific. Aaron writes like he's talking to a peer he respects, not an audience he's performing for. No gap between the person and the writing.

---

## What to Do

**Lead with what the thing is and how it works.** Not why it's great. Not what problem it solves in the abstract. What it actually does, concretely. His Reddit post opened with the game mechanic, the progression method, the WPM range. That's the model.

**Use specific numbers and details instead of adjectives.** "80 levels, 8 boss battles" beats "a comprehensive experience." "Starts at 20 WPM" beats "challenging for all skill levels." Specifics do the work adjectives pretend to do.

**Short paragraphs.** One idea, done, next. No multi-clause sentences straining to sound thorough.

**Dry humor, once, briefly.** A parenthetical aside or quick self-aware note is fine. "jk, I'm just jealous of anyone that fast" is Aaron. Move on. Don't repeat the joke, don't perform it.

**Be honest about limitations.** Aaron wrote "current version is copy only, but lots more in the works." He didn't oversell. Don't oversell on his behalf.

**Give credit genuinely.** When others contributed, say so plainly. No corporate-speak about "our amazing community."

**Use concrete analogies from everyday machines.** One per piece, used to explain, not decorate. The pattern: expensive-vs-adequate ("a V12 F1 engine sitting on a stand won't do the day-to-day work of a working Honda Civic"). If an analogy makes the thesis impossible to misread, use it.

**Close with a curiosity hook, not a naked link.** A question that hands the reader into the linked content beats dropping a URL. Still no pitch: "Curious what it actually does? In the post below..."

**Lead with what your starting assumptions got wrong.** Learning-in-public framing. "Be honest about limitations" applies to openers too: the wrong assumption you held is often the first paragraph.

**Name the actual tool or product** even when writing personally. Don't anonymize things readers would want to search for.

**Negation is allowed when it's a real concession.** The blocklist targets the rhetorical tic ("It's not just X, it's Y"). Honest concessions like "I'm not saying the models are comparable, but..." are fine.

**Close with community or next steps, not a pitch.** No "act now," no urgency theater. End like a person, not a funnel.

**Peer-to-peer, not brand-to-customer.** Even when writing for a business, the tone is person-to-person. He's talking to someone, not broadcasting at them.

**Use "I" when he means "I."** When Aaron is writing as himself, it's I. "We" is fine when there are multiple people involved, but don't hide behind it.

---

## What Not to Do

**No em dashes.** Never. Rewrite the sentence instead.

**No job titles.** Don't list what people's roles were. Leave it to the reader.

**No superlatives.** Not incredible, amazing, game-changing, remarkable, transformative, or any variant.

**No vague benefit statements.** "Helps you grow," "takes your X to the next level," "empowers your team" — none of it. If you can't say what it specifically does, don't say it.

**No throat-clearing.** Don't warm up before the point. Start with the point.

**No fake urgency or FOMO.** No limited-time framing, no "don't miss out," no artificial scarcity unless it's real.

**No dramatic one-sentence kickers** at the end of paragraphs. If the paragraph needs a mic drop, rewrite it.

**No numbered lists** unless the reader needs the items in order. Structure for clarity, not for the appearance of thoroughness.

**No broad world-state openers.** Don't start with "In today's rapidly changing business environment..." or anything like it. Start with the actual subject.

## Hard Word/Phrase Blocklist

Never use these in Aaron's voice:

**Slop phrases:** landscape, paradigm, leverage (verb), robust, seamless, ecosystem, holistic, nuanced, compelling, innovative, crucial, essential, fundamental, delve, navigate (metaphorically), unlock (metaphorically), double-edged sword, at the intersection of, in today's world, here's the thing, at its core, at the end of the day, let's dive in, make no mistake, perhaps most importantly, the bottom line, in other words, the reality is, spoiler alert, let that sink in

**Patterns:** "It's not just X — it's Y" / "This isn't just X, it's Z" / "The question isn't whether X, but Y" / "X is more than just Y" / any negation-then-assertion structure

**Full extended blocklist:** `channels/marketing/references/unslop-writing.md`

---

## The Test

Before publishing anything in Aaron's voice: does this sound like something a sharp, experienced person would say to a colleague over coffee? If it sounds like a brochure, a press release, or a LinkedIn ghostwriter, rewrite it.

## Example (Morse Command Reddit post, his actual voice)

> Built an asteroid shooter that teaches Morse code via Koch method. Starts at 20 WPM (yes that's high, but so is the fun), progresses through the alphabet. 80 levels, 8 boss battles. Current version is copy only, but lots more in the works.
> 73 de K1AXP

That's it. Mechanic first, specifics, dry aside in parens, honest about scope, signs off like a ham. No pitch.
