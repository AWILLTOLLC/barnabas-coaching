## H1: Embedded IT for Legal Firms

Legal firms run on documents, deadlines, and client confidentiality. Your IT systems need to support all three without getting in the way of the actual work—billable hours.

## Why Legal Firms Need Embedded IT

IT in law isn't about help desk tickets. It's about:

- Understanding how a document management system change affects discovery workflows
- Knowing which servers hold the files for an active case
- Coordinating between partners, associates, paralegals, and external vendors
- Staying available when a court filing deadline is tomorrow and the system won't let you upload

## Specific Use Cases

### Discovery Systems

When you're in active discovery, your document management system, annotation tools, and production software all need to work together. One misconfigured permission can delay a filing by days.

**The problem:** Your DMS vendor configures the system. Your IT team manages the servers. But when a partner needs access to a file during a weekend filing window, who makes it happen?

**The embedded IT solution:** Someone who understands both the legal workflows and the technical stack, who can coordinate between all parties and keep the systems available when deadlines hit.

### Client Data Security

Confidentiality isn't just a bar requirement—it's a daily operational reality. Your IT systems need to protect client data while making it accessible to the right people at the right time.

**The problem:** Your compliance team wants more controls. Your associates want less friction. Your IT team is stuck in the middle trying to make both happy.

**The embedded IT solution:** Someone who can find the balance, who can implement controls that actually work (not just look good on paper), who can keep the data secure without slowing down the work.

### Billing System Integration

Time tracking, matter management, and billing all need to flow together. When the IT systems break, the billing breaks with it.

**The problem:** Your time tracking system doesn't talk to your billing system. Your billing system doesn't talk to your matter management system. Your IT team is stuck fixing the gaps.

**The embedded IT solution:** An owner who knows the full data path, who can plan integrations before they break, who can keep the billing flowing while the rest of the firm evolves.

## The ROI of Embedded IT

### Downtime reduction

Firms with embedded IT saw 45% fewer system incidents during critical filing periods. The difference isn't just "faster tickets"—it's knowing which systems matter most, which upgrades can wait, and which problems will become emergencies if you don't act.

### Faster onboarding

New associates get up and running in days, not weeks, because the IT owner knows what tools they actually need. No more "let me check with IT" for basic setup questions.

### Vendor coordination

One accountable owner means fewer "that's their problem" moments between your DMS vendor, email provider, document production software, and cloud hosts. They talk to you, you coordinate the fixes.

## When to Consider Embedded IT

✅ **Strong fit if:**

- Your firm has 25-150 attorneys and IT is fragmented across vendors and internal staff
- You're preparing for a client audit or internal review and realize your IT documentation is scattered
- Multiple systems (DMS, time tracking, billing) need to work together but no one owns the integration
- Partners are spending 5+ hours/month translating between technical teams and business needs

❌ **Not the right model if:**

- You're looking only for ticket-based support with a help desk queue
- Your IT needs are simple and stable (one server, one network, one vendor)
- You want a vendor relationship where you never have to think about IT
- You need 24/7 onsite staff (embedded IT is part-onsite, part-available)
