# Why SMBs Need Embedded IT vs. Managed Services

**Purpose:** Thought leadership blog post for merkleandbloom.com  
**Target audience:** SMB owners, operations leaders, tech-savvy founders  
**Word count:** ~1,400 words

## The IT model most SMBs use is broken

You have two choices for IT: managed services or break/fix. Both have problems.

Managed services companies sell you a monthly retainer for "24/7 support." What you get is a help desk that closes tickets faster than they solve them. Your problems don't disappear—they just get assigned a number.

Break/fix is what it sounds like: call when things break, pay when they fix them. The problem is you don't know things are broken until they are. By then, you've lost data, lost time, lost customers.

## What embedded IT actually is

Embedded IT is neither. It's senior technical leadership that works on site with your team, understands the full environment, stays available when it matters, and takes responsibility for moving it forward.

Think of it like this: managed services is a vendor. Embedded IT is an owner.

A vendor sees your IT as a portfolio of systems to keep running. An owner sees it as a business asset that needs to move forward.

## The difference in practice

### Managed services: "That's not in your scope"

Your CRM isn't talking to your email system. Your ticket queue goes to the managed services company. They say it's not in their scope. You pay extra to get someone to look at it. They say it's not their problem. You're stuck.

### Embedded IT: "Here's what we're doing about it"

Your CRM isn't talking to your email system. The embedded IT owner looks at the full picture. They know which systems matter most. They know which gaps are costing you money. They coordinate between your vendors and your team to close the gaps.

### Managed services: "Ticket closed"

Your network is slow. You call the help desk. They restart the router. The ticket closes. You're happy for an hour. Then it happens again.

### Embedded IT: "Here's what we found"

Your network is slow. The embedded IT owner looks at the full picture. They find the switch that's failing. They coordinate the replacement before it goes down. They update the documentation so the next person knows what happened.

## When each model works

### Managed services works when:

- Your IT needs are simple and stable
- You want predictable monthly costs
- Your team doesn't have strong technical opinions
- You're okay with "good enough"

### Embedded IT works when:

- Your IT is mission-critical to your business
- You have strong technical people who need support, not management
- Your systems are complex and evolving
- You want someone who takes ownership, not just maintenance

## The hidden costs of managed services

### 1. The "not in scope" tax

Every time something falls between your vendors, you pay for it. Extra hours to figure out who owns the problem. Extra time to coordinate the fix. Extra frustration when things break.

### 2. The documentation gap

Managed services companies close tickets. They don't write down what they did. Six months later, you're back to square one when the same problem happens again.

### 3. The context problem

Your managed services team knows your systems. They don't know your business. They know how to restart the server. They don't know why the server matters to your customers.

## The embedded IT advantage

### 1. Context matters

An embedded IT owner sits in your office. They meet with your team. They understand your business. When a system goes down, they know which customers are affected and which deadlines are at risk.

### 2. Ownership vs. maintenance

Managed services keeps the lights on. Embedded IT moves the business forward. They're not just fixing problems—they're planning for growth, for new systems, for the next stage of your business.

### 3. One accountable owner

When something breaks, you know who to call. When you need to upgrade a system, you know who to talk to. When you're planning for growth, you know who to bring in early.

## A practical example

### The scenario

A 50-person biotech firm is growing fast. Their IT is fragmented: one vendor for their LIMS, another for their network, another for their email. Things work "most of the time." But leadership is spending 10+ hours/month translating between vendors.

### The managed services approach

They hire a managed services company. The help desk answers tickets. Problems get closed. But the LIMS still isn't talking to their quality system. The network still slows down during peak hours. The vendors still point at each other.

### The embedded IT approach

They bring in an embedded IT owner. The owner looks at the full picture. They find the gaps between systems. They coordinate with the vendors to close them. They build a documentation system so the next person knows what happened. They plan for the next stage of growth.

### The result

After six months, the firm has fewer system incidents. Leadership is spending 5 hours/month on IT instead of 10. The vendors talk to the owner, not to each other. And when the firm grows to 75 employees, the IT owner knows how to scale the systems without breaking them.

## When to consider embedded IT

✅ **Strong fit if:**

- Your IT is mission-critical to your business
- You have 20-200 employees
- Your systems are complex and evolving
- Leadership is spending 10+ hours/month on IT coordination
- You want someone who takes ownership, not just maintenance

❌ **Not the right model if:**

- Your IT needs are simple and stable
- You're looking for the lowest monthly cost
- You want a vendor relationship where you never have to think about IT
- You need 24/7 onsite staff (embedded IT is part-onsite, part-available)
