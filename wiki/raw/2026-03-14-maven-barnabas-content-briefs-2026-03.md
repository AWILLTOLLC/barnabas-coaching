## Brief 1: "AI for Small Business: What Actually Works in 2026"

**Target keyword:** `ai for small business`
**Monthly search volume:** 5,000 | **Competition:** Low (28/100)
**Intent:** Informational — someone trying to understand AI's role in their business
**Funnel stage:** TOFU (awareness)
**Goal:** Rank for the highest-volume term in our set, build authority, capture email

### Title Tag (≤60 characters)
```
AI for Small Business: What Actually Works in 2026
```

### Meta Description (≤160 characters)
```
Not hype. Not a sales pitch. An honest breakdown of what AI can actually do for small business owners right now — from someone who's seen it work.
```

### H1
`AI for Small Business: What Actually Works in 2026`

### Outline
1. **The problem with most AI advice** (short — 1-2 paragraphs)
   - Everything is either breathless hype or doom. Neither helps you run a business.
   - Aaron's POV: 25 years in tech, watched the hype cycles. Here's what's real.

2. **What AI is actually good at right now**
   - Admin and ops: scheduling, summarizing, drafting
   - Customer communication: first-draft emails, FAQs, response templates
   - Research and synthesis: competitive intel, market research, document review
   - Use real examples. Be specific. "A 12-person accounting firm uses it to draft client update emails — saves 45 min/day."

3. **What AI is NOT good at (yet)**
   - Judgment calls that require context
   - Anything client-facing without human review
   - Replacing domain expertise
   - This section builds trust. Say what the tools can't do.

4. **The 3 questions to ask before adopting any AI tool**
   - What problem am I actually solving?
   - Who on my team owns this?
   - How will I know if it's working?

5. **Where to start if you're overwhelmed**
   - Pick one use case. Run it for 30 days. Measure.
   - CTA: "If you want help figuring out which use case fits your business, that's exactly what we do. Book a free 30-minute call."

### Word count target
1,200–1,600 words. Dense enough to be useful, tight enough to finish.

### Internal links to add
- Link to the existing "5 AI Mistakes" post in section 3 ("What AI is NOT good at")
- Link to the Services/Contact page in the CTA

### Notes
- Do NOT write this as a listicle of AI tools. That's not the angle.
- Aaron's voice: direct, honest, operator-perspective. He's not selling AI. He's been in rooms where it worked and rooms where it didn't.
- Include one concrete story or example from a real business context (can be anonymized).

## SEO Reference: What Barrett Needs to Know

### The basics of ranking a page

**Google ranks pages, not websites.** Each post competes independently. A great post on a weak domain can still rank if the topic is low-competition and the content is genuinely useful.

**Targeting a keyword means:**
1. Using the exact phrase (or very close variant) in: title tag, H1, and naturally in the first 100 words
2. Writing content that actually answers what the person searching that term wants to know
3. Not using it so many times it looks forced — 3-5 uses in a 1,200-word post is plenty

**Title tag** = what shows up in Google search results as the blue link. ≤60 characters. Most important SEO element on the page. Should include the target keyword.

**Meta description** = the grey text under the blue link. Doe
