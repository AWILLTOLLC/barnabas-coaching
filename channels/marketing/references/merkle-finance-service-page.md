## H1: Embedded IT for Financial Services Firms

Financial services firms operate under constant scrutiny: SEC regulations, audit trails that span years, trading systems that can't afford downtime, and client data that needs to be both accessible and protected.

## Why Finance Firms Need Embedded IT

IT in finance isn't about tickets and tickets closed. It's about:

- Understanding how a system change affects your SEC reporting
- Knowing which servers hold the data you need for an audit
- Coordinating between compliance, trading, and operations without breaking any of their workflows
- Staying available when the market opens and your systems need to be perfect

## Specific Use Cases

### SEC Compliance Systems

FINRA rules, SEC Rule 17a-4, audit trail requirements—these aren't IT problems, they're business requirements that IT needs to execute.

**The problem:** Your compliance team knows what the SEC requires. Your IT team knows how to configure the servers. But when an auditor asks "show me the audit trail for this transaction," who connects the dots?

**The embedded IT solution:** Someone who understands both the regulatory requirements and the technical implementation, who can keep the systems compliant without turning them into black boxes.

### Trading System Infrastructure

When your trading platform goes down, it's not just "IT downtime." It's missed opportunities, client frustration, and potentially regulatory questions. The IT owner who knows your trading stack can prevent problems before they happen.

**The problem:** Your trading platform has dependencies on your network, your authentication system, your data feeds. When one breaks, they all break. Who owns the whole picture?

**The embedded IT solution:** An owner who knows the full stack, who can plan for single points of failure, who can keep the systems running when the market opens.

### Client Data Management

HIPAA meets SEC compliance meets client confidentiality. Your IT systems need to handle all three without over-complicating the user experience for your analysts.

**The problem:** Your compliance team wants more controls. Your analysts want less friction. Your IT team is stuck in the middle trying to make both happy.

**The embedded IT solution:** Someone who can find the balance, who can implement controls that actually work (not just look good on paper), who can keep the data secure without slowing down the work.

## The ROI of Embedded IT

### Compliance confidence

When your IT owner knows your regulatory requirements, audit season goes from panic to "we've got this." You spend less time scrambling for documentation and more time proving what you've built.

### System reliability

Firms with embedded IT saw 35% fewer system incidents that affected trading or client reporting. The difference isn't just "faster fixes"—it's knowing which systems matter most, which upgrades can wait, and which problems will become emergencies if you don't act.

### Faster onboarding

New analysts get up and running in days, not weeks, because the IT owner knows what tools they actually need. No more "let me check with IT" for basic setup questions.

## When to Consider Embedded IT

✅ **Strong fit if:**

- Your firm has 20-100 employees and IT is fragmented across vendors and internal staff
- You're preparing for an SEC or FINRA audit and realize your documentation is incomplete
- Multiple systems (portfolio management, CRM, compliance) need to work together but no one owns the integration
- Leadership is spending 10+ hours/month translating between technical teams and business needs

❌ **Not the right model if:**

- You're looking only for ticket-based support with a help desk queue
- Your IT needs are simple and stable (one server, one network, one vendor)
- You want a vendor relationship where you never have to think about IT
- You need 24/7 onsite staff (embedded IT is part-onsite, part-available)
