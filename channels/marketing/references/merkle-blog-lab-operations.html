Your Lab's IT Is Not Just IT

In a lab, IT isn't about helping people check email. It's about keeping research moving. It's about GxP compliance. It's about instrument connectivity. It's about data integrity.

A server going down in an office means someone can't send an email. A server going down in a lab means months of data could be lost.

The unique IT challenges of labs

GxP compliance

GxP (Good Practice) requirements mean your IT systems need to be more than "working." They need to be auditable. They need to have audit trails. They need to have controlled access. They need to have version history.

The problem: Your LIMS vendor configured the system for a different lab. Your IT team knows the servers but not the compliance requirements. Who owns the gap?

The embedded IT solution: Someone who knows both GxP requirements and your technical stack, who can coordinate between your compliance team and your IT team, who can keep the systems compliant without turning them into black boxes.

Instrument connectivity

Mass spectrometers, HPLC systems, automated sample processors—these instruments generate data that needs to flow into your LIMS. The network paths are often isolated. The protocols are often legacy. The support contracts often expire.

The problem: Your network team knows the switches but not the instruments. Your instrument vendor knows the machines but not your network. Who owns the connection?

The embedded IT solution: Someone who can bridge both worlds, who knows when to call the vendor and when to fix it themselves, who keeps the instruments online without clogging your internal support queue.

Data integrity

Your lab's data is its most valuable asset. A corrupted dataset means re-running experiments. A lost dataset means months of work gone. A misconfigured permission means a compliance finding.

The problem: Your IT team manages the servers. Your compliance team manages the requirements. Your researchers manage the data. Who owns the full picture?

The embedded IT solution: Someone who understands the full data path, who can plan for single points of failure, who can keep the data secure without slowing down the research.

24/7 availability

Research doesn't stop at 5pm. When a centrifuge goes offline at 2pm on a Friday, the samples keep spoiling. When a server goes down on a Saturday, the data migration keeps failing.

The problem: Your help desk closes at 5pm. Your instrument vendor shows up in two days. Your IT team is spread thin.

The embedded IT solution: Someone who stays available when it matters, who knows which problems are urgent, who can coordinate between your team and your vendors.

Specific use cases

LIMS (Laboratory Information Management System)

The LIMS is the heart of your lab. It tracks samples. It tracks results. It tracks compliance. It needs to be available. It needs to be accurate. It needs to be auditable.

Common problems:

  • Permissions are wrong, so the wrong people can see the wrong data
  • The audit trail is broken, so an auditor can't trace a result
  • The integration with instruments is broken, so data has to be entered manually
  • The backup is broken, so a corrupted database means lost data

The embedded IT solution: Someone who knows your LIMS, who knows your compliance requirements, who can coordinate with your vendor, who can keep the system running.

ELN (Electronic Lab Notebook)

The ELN is where researchers record their work. It needs to be easy to use. It needs to be searchable. It needs to be integrated with the LIMS. It needs to have version history.

Common problems:

  • Researchers don't use it because it's not integrated with their workflow
  • The search is broken, so they can't find previous experiments
  • The integration with the LIMS is broken, so data has to be entered twice
  • The version history is broken, so a corrupted notebook means lost data

The embedded IT solution: Someone who understands researcher workflows, who can coordinate with your vendor, who can keep the system usable.

Quality Management System (QMS)

The QMS tracks your quality processes: deviations, CAPAs, audits, training. It needs to be compliant. It needs to be accessible. It needs to be auditable.

Common problems:

  • The system is too complex, so researchers avoid it
  • The approval workflow is broken, so a CAPA gets stuck
  • The training records are broken, so an auditor can't verify competency
  • The integration with the LIMS is broken, so a deviation isn't linked to the affected samples

The embedded IT solution: Someone who understands quality workflows, who can coordinate with your compliance team, who can keep the system usable.

Instrument networks

Your instruments are on isolated VLANs. They have legacy protocols. They have support contracts that expire. They have dependencies on your network.

Common problems:

  • A switch fails, so all the instruments go offline
  • A protocol changes, so the instruments can't talk to the LIMS
  • A support contract expires, so the vendor takes weeks to show up
  • A firmware update breaks an integration

The embedded IT solution: Someone who knows the instruments, who knows your network, who can coordinate with your vendors, who can keep the instruments online.

The ROI of embedded IT for labs

Downtime reduction

Labs with embedded IT saw 40% fewer unplanned outages in year one. The difference isn't just "faster tickets"—it's knowing which systems matter most, which upgrades can wait, and which problems will become emergencies if you don't act.

Audit readiness

When the IT owner knows your workflows, audit prep goes from "where's the documentation?" to "here's what changed last quarter." You spend less time scrambling and more time proving compliance.

Faster onboarding

New researchers get up and running in days, not weeks, because the IT owner knows what tools they actually need. No more "let me check with IT" for basic setup questions.

Vendor coordination

One accountable owner means fewer "that's their problem" moments between your LIMS vendor, instrument manufacturers, network provider, and cloud hosts. They talk to you, you coordinate the fixes.

When to consider embedded IT for your lab

Strong fit if:

  • Your lab has grown past 10 employees but a full internal IT department feels premature
  • You're preparing for an audit and realize your IT documentation is scattered
  • Multiple systems (LIMS, ELN, instrument software) need to work together but no one owns the integration
  • Leadership is spending 10+ hours/month translating between technical teams and business needs
  • Your IT is mission-critical to your research

Not the right model if:

  • You're looking only for ticket-based support with a help desk queue
  • Your IT needs are simple and stable (one server, one network, one vendor)
  • You want a vendor relationship where you never have to think about IT
  • You need 24/7 onsite staff (embedded IT is part-onsite, part-available)
