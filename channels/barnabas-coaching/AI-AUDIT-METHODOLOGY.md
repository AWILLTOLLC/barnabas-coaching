# AI Audit Methodology — Barnabas Coaching
## Strategic Roadmap for On-Site Business Assessments

*Working document. For Aaron (and Michelle once she's running audits).*

---

## The Philosophy

Most AI consultants hand clients a list of tools. That's not an audit — that's a product catalog with a markup.

The Barnabas audit does two things: it tells a business owner **where AI can genuinely help them** and **what has to be true before that help is real**. Opportunity without readiness is just expensive experimentation.

The method is part structured interview, part ethnographic research. You're not asking people what their problems are — you're watching what their day actually looks like, then telling them what you saw.

---

## Engagement Overview

**Total timeline: 7–10 business days**

| Phase | When | Duration |
|-------|------|----------|
| 0. Pre-visit intake | Days 1–3 | ~1 hr owner / ~30 min Aaron |
| 1. On-site day | Day 4–5 | Half or full day (read the business) |
| 2. Synthesis & report | Days 5–7 post-visit | 3–5 hrs |
| 3. Working session | Days 8–10 | 90 min |

The on-site day is the center of gravity. Everything else serves it.

---

## Phase 0: Pre-Visit Intake

**Goal:** Arrive knowing the business well enough to ask good questions, not orientation questions.

**Owner sends before the visit:**
- Org chart (or hand-drawn equivalent — this is fine)
- Current software/tools list (even rough: "we use QuickBooks, Salesforce, and a bunch of spreadsheets")
- A short written prompt: *"Describe the three things that eat the most time in your business right now. Be specific."*

**Aaron does before the visit:**
- 20-minute review of their website, LinkedIn, any public presence
- Quick industry scan — what AI adoption looks like in their sector (are competitors already doing this?)
- Draft the on-site schedule based on intake; confirm with owner

**Pre-visit call (optional, 20 min):**
Use this if the intake questionnaire raises questions or the business is complex. Not required for most engagements.

## Phase 1: On-Site Day

### Participants
- Aaron (lead) + Michelle (ops/process lens) when available
- Owner required for opening and closing blocks
- 2–4 additional staff as owner designates (front-line workers, a department head, whoever owns a problem area)

### Structure

#### Block 1 — Owner Strategy Session (60–90 min)
Sit down with the owner first. This is not a tour; it's a briefing.

**Cover:**
- Business model: how do you make money, what's the unit of value?
- Revenue drivers: where does growth come from, where does it stall?
- Pain ranking: of the three things you wrote down, rank them by how much they cost you (time, money, or risk)
- AI awareness: have you tried anything? What happened? What are you skeptical about?
- Decision-making: who else in the org makes meaningful operational decisions?
- Budget reality: what's the real number you'd consider for a solution that worked? (don't skip this)
- Constraints: anything off-limits, politically sensitive, or already "decided"?

**What you're building:** a map of what the owner actually cares about, in order. The audit findings will be anchored to this.

---

#### Block 2 — Operational Walkthrough / "Follow Me" Sessions (2–3 hrs)

This is the highest-signal work of the day. You're watching, not interviewing.

**The request to staff:** *"Just do what you normally do. Pretend we're not here. We'll ask questions as we go."*

**What to shadow:**
- A sale from inquiry to close (or as much of it as fits)
- An order, job, or project from intake to delivery
- A customer service interaction (inbound inquiry, complaint, or follow-up)
- An admin task (invoicing, scheduling, reporting — whatever they do most)

**What you're documenting in real time:**

| Signal | What it looks like | Why it matters |
|--------|-------------------|----------------|
| Repetitive manual steps | Copy/paste, re-keying data, reformatting | Automation target |
| Tool-switching | Opens 4 apps to do one thing | Integration opportunity |
| Information hunting | "Let me find that..." takes > 60 seconds | Search/retrieval AI |
| Re-entry | Same data in multiple systems | Process debt + AI fix |
| Workarounds | Post-its, side spreadsheets, "we just text each other" | Hidden process gaps |
| Judgment calls | Approvals, exceptions, escalations | AI augmentation, not replacement |
| Communication overhead | Emails explaining the same thing repeatedly | Template/draft AI |
| Bottlenecks | "We wait for [person] to do [thing]" | Process redesign + AI |

**Michelle's role in this block (when present):** She takes the process/operations lens. Aaron takes the technology lens. Compare notes after — the gaps between what people say their process is and what it actually is will surface in that conversation.

---

#### Block 3 — Functional Mini-Interviews (30–45 min each, select 2–3)

Short, focused conversations with whoever owns a key function. Not a group session — individual or pairs.

**Standard functions to cover:**
- Sales / business development
- Customer service / client communication
- Operations / delivery / fulfillment
- Finance & admin
- Marketing (if they have someone doing it)
- HR / staffing (if 20+ employees)

**For each function, get:**
1. What's your job, in your own words?
2. What takes the most time that shouldn't?
3. What do you have to do manually that feels like it should be automatic?
4. What decisions do you make every day that follow a pattern?
5. What information do you wish you had but don't?
6. What would make you 20% more effective tomorrow if it just worked?

**Don't over-interview.** Two good functional sessions beat five rushed ones. If the business is 10 people, the owner may wear all these hats — adjust accordingly.

---

#### Block 4 — Tech Stack Inventory (30–45 min)

This can be done with the owner or an ops/admin person.

**Document:**
- Every software tool in use (including free tiers, trials, and "the spreadsheet")
- Which ones are actually used vs. theoretically in use
- Integrations that exist vs. gaps where data has to move manually
- Any data they're collecting that they're not using
- Where the "system of record" actually is for: customers, jobs/orders, communications, financials

**What you're assessing:**
- Data availability (is there data to work with?)
- Data quality (is it clean, consistent, accessible?)
- Integration surface (can AI tools plug in, or is this a data silo problem first?)
- Vendor lock-in (are they trapped in a platform that limits options?)

---

#### Block 5 — Synthesis (30–45 min, Aaron + Michelle only)

Before leaving the building or shortly after, debrief together.

**Answer these questions:**
1. What were the top 3 things we saw that were genuinely surprising?
2. What is the single highest-impact AI opportunity we identified?
3. What's the biggest readiness gap between what's possible and what they can actually execute?
4. Are there any opportunities we need to *not* recommend — because the risk, complexity, or org readiness isn't there?
5. What is the one thing the owner clearly cares most about that we need to anchor the report to?

Write these down. This becomes the spine of the report.

---

#### Block 6 — Owner Debrief (30 min, same day)

Before you leave, give the owner a verbal summary of what you saw. No recommendations yet — just observations.

*"Here's what we noticed. Here's where we saw the most friction. Here are the questions we're going to be sitting with as we write the report."*

This does two things: it validates your read of the business (they'll correct you if you're wrong), and it sets expectations that the real output comes in the working session, not today.

## Phase 2: Synthesis & Report

**Timeline:** Complete within 5 business days of the on-site visit. 7 max.

### Readiness Assessment

Score each dimension 1–5 before writing a word of the report. This forces honest calibration.

| Dimension | 1 (not ready) | 5 (ready to move) |
|-----------|--------------|-------------------|
| **Data maturity** | No data, siloed, or unreliable | Clean, accessible, structured data in usable systems |
| **Process maturity** | Undocumented, inconsistent, exception-heavy | Documented, repeatable, measurable |
| **Tech stack health** | Legacy, fragmented, no integration surface | Modern, integrated, API-accessible |
| **Org readiness** | Low tech literacy, change-resistant, no budget clarity | Tech-comfortable staff, change appetite, real budget |
| **Leadership commitment** | Checking a box | Genuinely committed and will champion change |

A business scoring 3+ across all dimensions is ready to act. Below that, readiness work comes before AI deployment — and the report needs to say so, plainly.

---

### Opportunity Scoring

For each identified AI opportunity, score before ranking:

| Factor | Score (1–3) |
|--------|------------|
| Impact if it works | 1=marginal, 2=meaningful, 3=transformative |
| Implementation complexity | 1=high, 2=medium, 3=low (inverted — lower complexity = higher score) |
| Time to value | 1=>12 months, 2=3–12 months, 3=<3 months |
| Data readiness for this specific use | 1=not there, 2=partial, 3=ready now |

**Priority score = sum of above.** 10–12: lead with it. 7–9: include with conditions. Below 7: document but don't recommend yet.

---

### Report Structure

**Length: 8–12 pages.** Owner-readable. Not a whitepaper.

**Section 1 — Executive Summary (1 page)**
What we found. What we recommend. In that order. No setup, no methodology, no caveats. The owner should be able to read this and brief their spouse on it over dinner.

**Section 2 — What We Observed (1–2 pages)**
Business overview as understood post-audit. Current state of operations, tech stack, and team. Written in plain language — this section validates to the owner that you actually understood their business. If it's wrong, they'll tell you.

**Section 3 — AI Readiness Assessment (1 page)**
The scored dimensions (1–5) with 1–2 sentences of explanation per dimension. Be direct. If data quality is a 2, say: *"Your customer data lives in three places and none of them agree. Before AI can help you here, you need a single source of truth."*

**Section 4 — Opportunity Map (2–3 pages)**
Prioritized list of AI opportunities. For each:
- What it is (plain English, one sentence)
- What problem it solves (anchored to something the owner said they care about)
- What it requires to work
- Rough effort and cost to implement
- Expected time to see results
- Specific tools or vendors worth evaluating (2–3 max per opportunity — not a catalog)

Visual format works well here: a 2x2 of Impact vs. Complexity, then detailed writeups below.

**Section 5 — What We're Not Recommending (and Why) (½ page)**
This section is the brand differentiator. Most consultants don't include it. Aaron does.

Explicitly call out 2–3 things that might seem like AI opportunities but aren't — for this business, at this time. *"Predictive analytics could theoretically help your sales forecasting — but you don't have 24 months of clean pipeline data yet. This is a 2027 conversation, not a 2026 one."*

This builds trust faster than anything else in the report.

**Section 6 — Implementation Roadmap (1 page)**
Three columns: Now (0–90 days), Next (3–6 months), Later (6–18 months).

Specific, sequenced, realistic. Not aspirational. If something in the "Later" column depends on something in "Now" being done first, say so.

**Section 7 — Your Questions, Answered (optional, ½ page)**
If the owner raised specific questions or skepticisms during the audit, address them here directly. *"You asked whether AI could replace your dispatcher. Short answer: no, and you shouldn't want it to. Here's what it can do instead..."*

## Phase 3: Working Session

**Format:** 90-minute video call or in-person (owner's preference). Aaron leads; Michelle joins.

**Agenda:**
- 20 min: Walk through the Opportunity Map together. Not reading the report — discussing it.
- 20 min: Owner reacts, corrects, reorders priorities. This is normal and expected; the working session is where you refine, not defend.
- 20 min: Go deep on the top 1–2 opportunities. What does implementation actually look like? Who does what?
- 15 min: Agree on immediate next steps (3–5 actions, owner-owned)
- 15 min: Discuss whether a coaching retainer makes sense — what they'd get, how it would work

**One output from this session:** a one-page "agreed next steps" doc, sent within 24 hours. Not a proposal — a record of what the owner said they were going to do and what Barnabas committed to supporting.

## The Repeatable Core: Industry-Agnostic Questions

These work in any business. Pull from them during interviews and follow-me sessions.

### On time and labor:
- Where does your team spend time that doesn't create value for the customer?
- What do people do more than once that could be done once and reused?
- What's the last thing that fell through the cracks, and why?

### On information:
- When you need to know something about a customer/job/order, how long does it take to find out?
- What reports or summaries do you wish existed but don't?
- What data are you collecting that nobody ever looks at?

### On communication:
- How many of your emails could be templates?
- How often do people ask you questions that have the same answer every time?
- Where does miscommunication between your team create rework?

### On decisions:
- What decisions get made the same way every time, but still require a person to make them?
- What would let your best employee teach their judgment to someone new?
- Where do you slow down because you're waiting for information?

### On customers:
- What do customers ask you most often that you could answer faster?
- What part of the customer experience could be better but isn't because it takes too much time?
- What do customers complain about that's really an internal process problem?

## Pricing & Scope Alignment

The $2,500 AI Audit is well-positioned for this methodology. The 30-day window on the site can be updated to reflect the real timeline (7–10 business days), which is a feature, not a liability — it signals efficiency and respect for the owner's time.
