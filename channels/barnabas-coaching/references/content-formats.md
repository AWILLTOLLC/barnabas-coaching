# Barnabas Coaching — Content Format Library

_For Barrett. Execution-level playbook. No need to ping Maven for standard content._

---

## Brand Voice Snapshot

Barnabas Coaching speaks with restrained authority. The voice is the experienced operator who's been in the room — not the vendor who wants to sell you something. Sober, precise, and honest about what AI can and can't do. Zero hype. Zero buzzwords. Zero "AI will change everything" framing. Sound like the ex-Microsoft engineer who's seen fifteen enterprise technology waves and knows which ones actually changed the floor. The brand is Aaron's credibility made accessible to Seattle SMBs. Never sound like a consultant. Sound like a trusted colleague.

---

## Audience Primer

**Who they are:** Seattle-area small-to-mid business owners, 5–200 employees. Owner-operated or founder-led. Aware that AI is happening and worried they're being left behind. Not technical. They've heard the hype and don't fully trust it, but they also don't want to miss a real opportunity.

**What they care about:** Not wasting money on tools that don't actually work. Practical outcomes, not frameworks. Whether their competitors are doing this already. Time — they are busy and don't want a 10-week consulting engagement to figure out a basic question.

**What language they use:** "My team," "our workflow," "we tried [tool] and it didn't stick," "ROI," "bandwidth," "figuring out where to even start." They say "AI tools" not "LLMs." They are not intimidated by jargon but they don't use it themselves. They respond to specificity ("this saved a biotech firm 12 hours/week on documentation") over generality ("AI increases efficiency").

## Content Format Library

### LinkedIn (Aaron's Personal Profile)

---

**Format: The Honest AI Take**
- **When to use:** 2–3x per week. This is the core format. The whole marketing strategy is Aaron being the honest voice in a room full of hype.
- **Structure:** Hook (first line, no preamble): one specific, contrarian or honest observation about AI in business → 3–5 short paragraphs of real reasoning (no lists yet) → pull-quote moment: one line that would make someone stop scrolling → optional CTA: "If you're a Seattle business owner trying to figure out where AI actually fits — that's the conversation I have for a living. Link in bio."
- **Example:**
  > Most AI implementations fail not because the tool is bad, but because no one mapped the actual workflow before buying it.
  >
  > I've watched companies spend $50k on an AI platform and then realize three months later that the problem they were solving with it takes 20 minutes a week.
  >
  > The question isn't "what AI tool should we use." It's "where are the 10-hour-per-week problems in your business?" Start there. The tool choice becomes obvious.
  >
  > 25 years of watching tech come and go taught me that the implementation gap is always the story. Not the technology.

---

**Format: The Specific Win (Case Study Fragment)**
- **When to use:** Monthly or after any client engagement produces a concrete outcome. Social proof is the fastest trust-builder at this stage.
- **Structure:** Hook: one specific, quantified outcome → "Here's what that actually looked like:" → 3–4 short paragraphs telling the actual story (anonymized if needed): the problem, what they tried, what was implemented, what changed → ending: "This is the work." → optional CTA for discovery call.
- **Example:**
  > A 12-person professional services firm was spending 8 hours/week writing client-facing meeting summaries. We fixed that in a single afternoon.
  >
  > The solution wasn't a fancy platform. It was a well-structured prompt, a $20/month ChatGPT license, and a 30-minute training session.
  >
  > That's $40k+ of annual labor time recaptured. The firm's principal used those hours to take on a new client.
  >
  > Not every AI win is complicated. Sometimes it's just knowing which tool to reach for and how to use it.

---

**Format: The Myth-Busting Post**
- **When to use:** When a new AI hype cycle peaks (product launches, news events, viral AI moments). Timely content that positions Aaron as the sober take.
- **Structure:** Hook: name the myth directly → "Here's what's actually true:" → walk through the reality in concrete terms → end with the practical implication for a Seattle SMB owner.
- **Example:**
  > Everyone's talking about [new AI tool]. Here's what a Seattle business owner actually needs to know about it.
  >
  > The capability is real. The 10x productivity claim is not — at least not yet, not for most workflows, and not without significant setup investment.
  >
  > Here's what I'd actually evaluate before spending money on it: [3 specific questions].
  >
  > Hype cycles are useful for keeping track of what's emerging. They're dangerous when they drive purchasing decisions.

---

**Format: The Behind-The-Scenes Credibility Post**
- **When to use:** Once a month. Lets the audience see the team and the actual work.
- **Structure:** Something true and specific from a recent engagement or internal work → no performance, no polish → ends with a genuine thought or observation, not a CTA.
- **Example:**
  > Spent yesterday afternoon doing a workflow audit with a client. Three hours in a conference room, just mapping where their team's time actually goes.
  >
  > The AI tools came up in hour two. Before that, we were just talking about the business.
  >
  > That's usually where the real work starts.

---

**Format: The Seattle Business Community Post**
- **When to use:** After local events, meetups, or any engagement with the Seattle business community.
- **Structure:** Name the event or context → one observation from the room → connect it to something Barnabas is working on → tag relevant people or organizations.
- **Example:**
  > Was at [Seattle Chamber / local event] last week. Room full of business owners asking the same question: "how do I know which AI tools are actually worth it?"
  >
  > The honest answer: most of them aren't, for most workflows. But the ones that are can be transformative.
  >
  > That gap — between hype and actual utility — is exactly why we built Barnabas.

### Email / Direct Outreach

- **When to use:** For warm outreach to SMB owners in Aaron's existing network or in target industries.

  > Aaron

## What Never To Do

1. **Never use AI buzzword language.** "Leverage AI capabilities," "unlock the power of machine learning," "transform your digital journey" — these are trust destroyers for a sophisticated B2B audience. Aaron's credibility is built on saying what other consultants won't. Kill every buzzword on sight.
2. **Never be vague about outcomes.** "We help businesses be more efficient" is worthless. "We found a Seattle professional services firm was spending 8 hours/week on a task that took 20 minutes after a single process change" is credible. Every claim needs a number or a story behind it.
3. **Never post more than Aaron can authentically own.** If Barrett generates content that Aaron then has to pretend is his genuine take, the whole strategy breaks. Every post should read like it came directly from Aaron's brain. When in doubt, ask Aaron to review before publishing.
4. **Never lead with the product before building trust.** Barnabas is pre-revenue. The discovery call pipeline comes from LinkedIn authority, not LinkedIn ads. Do not start pitching the $2,500 audit to an audience that doesn't know who Aaron is yet. Build the voice first, convert second.
5. **Never compete with the hype machine.** Don't post about the latest AI product release as if you're excited about it. The whole positioning is the honest counter-voice. Stay in that lane even when it's tempting to post timely hype content.
